01 / Understand

What keeps waterways healthy?

Forests, wetlands and soil affect how rain moves through an ecosystem.

🌧️ RainWater reaches the land
→
🌿 InfiltrationWater soaks into soil
↔
🌊 RunoffWater flows over land
→
🟢 StorageSoils and wetlands hold water
🌳

Forests

Roots help hold soil together. Plants slow surface runoff and reduce erosion.

🪷

Wetlands

Wetlands store water, slow flow, trap some sediment and provide habitats.

🪱

Healthy soil

Soils allow water to infiltrate and store moisture for plants.

Remember: Erosion means soil is worn away and moved. Sediment washed into streams can make water cloudy and harm aquatic habitats.
02 / Investigate

What is damaging the waterway?

Choose a problem to see how it can affect water movement, water quality and biodiversity. Use one as the focus of your presentation.

Human activity

Land clearing

Removing native vegetation can expose soil and reduce the number of roots holding it together.

1 · ActivityTrees are removed
2 · ChangeMore erosion and sediment runoff
3 · ConsequenceCloudier water and damaged habitats
Good science sentence: “Because ______ changes ______, this can lead to ______, which affects ______.”

Extra pressure: drought can reduce water availability, but drought itself is not a human activity.

04 / Prove it

What evidence shows restoration can work?

Choose one real New Zealand project for Slide 4. The key is to explain what changed, rather than only how many trees were planted.

32%
less fine sediment

In restored streams compared with open-pasture streams

Mahurangi catchment restoration

A 2023–2025 study compared nine small streams. Fenced, replanted streams had about 32% less fine sediment than open-pasture streams. Sensitive mayflies and caddisflies were also found at restored sites.

Be careful: The study compared different streams. It supports restoration, but cannot prove planting alone caused every difference.
Read the Auckland Council study ↗
What counts as strong evidence?

Changes in sediment, nutrient levels, water clarity, native plants or freshwater species. Repeated measurements before and after restoration are especially helpful.
